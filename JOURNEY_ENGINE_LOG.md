# Journey Engine V2: journey log

The record the V2 handoff asks for (sections 3.9, 7 and 10): for each journey
attempted, its test group, mood and audience, the peak and why, what was
checked and what's still uncertain, a self-check against the quality gate,
and the candidates turned down and why. It's the raw material for the
evaluation report (27 Nov 2026): *Journey Engine V2 Evaluation Report*, a Claude
Doc at https://claude.ai/artifact/CXo7TVTup9nKmHfqRgrQpr

The scores are Claude's own check against the handoff's weights, not the
independent review it asks for: each journey still needs that, and ideally a
walk.

Photo treatment for every journey: `main-app/photo-originals/` (see
"Photo treatment" in the README).

## 1. The Carriage Road at Dusk (`marunouchi`), the gold standard

- **Test group:** Tokyo, Japan: a romantic journey for a couple.
- **Status:** made and in the app (26 Nov 2026). Not yet walked.
- **Primary mood:** Romantic (secondary: Cultural). **Audience:** a couple.
- **Tags:** Scenic, Historical, Cultural, Interactive.
- **Route:** Hibiya Park (Departure) → Nijūbashi (Discovery) → Wadakura
  Fountain Park (Build) → KITTE Garden (Peak) → Tokyo Station dome
  (Afterglow). One-way, 2.6 km, all flat, about 2½ hours. Starts at Hibiya
  Station, ends at Tokyo Station.
- **Peak, and why it was chosen first:** the red-brick Marunouchi building
  lit up at dusk, from KITTE's free rooftop garden. It's free, open until
  10 or 11 PM, reached by lift, and it's the strongest romantic view in
  central Tokyo that doesn't need a ticket or a booking. The route was built
  back from it along Gyōkō-dōri, the road the ambassadors' carriages take.
- **The thread:** letters and promises kept. A ginkgo saved by a man who
  bet his neck on it, the ambassadors' letters of credence, fountains built
  for two royal weddings, a station rebuilt to its 1914 self, and a letter
  for the couple to write in the old postmaster's room and post.
- **Optional activity:** writing each other a letter in KITTE's old
  postmaster's room (kept by KITTE as "a place to write to someone you're
  thinking of"), and posting it.
- **Nearby discoveries researched:** the Crane Fountain and Matsumoto-rō
  (Hibiya Park), the Intermediatheque (KITTE), Naka-dōri's winter lights
  (by the station). None at Nijūbashi or Wadakura worth a detour at dusk:
  the East Gardens close in the afternoon.
- **Checked against:** Ministry of the Environment (Kokyo Gaien: Nijūbashi,
  the black pines, Wadakura Fountain Park's history and its fountain and
  light-up hours, toilets, access); Tokyo Metropolitan Park Association
  (Hibiya Park: opening, the Kubikake ginkgo, the rebuilding); KITTE's own
  site (garden and postmaster's room hours); the Intermediatheque's own site;
  Ministry of Foreign Affairs, Diplomatic Bluebook 2005 (the carriages, the
  red carpet, about 40 ambassadors a year); Norimono News (2018) on the
  carriage route and the 1913 carriage; published accounts of the station's
  1945 damage and 2012 restoration, and of the 1959 royal wedding; Japan
  Post's rates (October 2024). Coordinates from OpenStreetMap; walking
  routes from Google's Routes API; heights from GSI.
- **Uncertain, and marked so:**
  - the station's light-up hours ("sunset to about 9 PM" by most accounts;
    no official source found);
  - Marunouchi Illumination 2026's hours (dates are out, hours weren't);
  - "about forty ambassadors a year" is from 2005;
  - KITTE's lifts to the 4th and 6th floors (not checked on its site);
  - "eight of the twelve zodiac animals" in the domes rests on one source.
  Left out entirely: the Rokumeikan site marker (removed in 2022 for
  redevelopment), and the year the post office was finished (sources say
  1931 or 1933).
- **Hard-fail check:** none. Every stop is real and free to enter; the peak
  is reachable by lift until late; the timing works from about an hour
  before sunset in any season; the photos are real, of these places, and
  licensed (CC BY / CC BY-SA, credited); the gravel plaza is flagged for
  strollers and wheelchairs.
- **Self-check scores (handoff weights):** distinctive discoveries 22/25,
  emotional arc and peak 23/25, route and practical 18/20, storytelling
  14/15, hero and presentation 8/10, audience and mood fit 5/5. **90/100.**
  Held back by: a hero that's dusk rather than full night, stop photos from
  mixed seasons, and two unconfirmed hours.
- **Candidates turned down:**
  - *Kagurazaka to the Canal Café on the outer moat:* the peak would have
    been a restaurant, which the handoff only allows as a core stop when
    it's essential to the story.
  - *Asakusa, along the Sumida, to Tokyo Skytree at night:* a paid,
    crowded peak, and a weaker thread than the carriage road.

## 2. Seven Bridges to the Ridge (`duluthRomantic`)

- **Test group:** Duluth, couples only: the Romantic one of three.
- **Status:** made and in the app (26 Nov 2026). Not yet walked or driven.
- **Primary mood:** Romantic (secondary: Peaceful). **Audience:** a couple.

- **Route:** the Lester River's mouth (Departure) → Lester Park's falls
  (Discovery) → Seven Bridges Road (Build) → Hawk Ridge (Peak) → Brighton
  Beach (Afterglow). By car, about 12 km of driving, with short walks at
  each stop. Best in the last two hours before sunset, late August to
  October.
- **Peak, and why:** the whole North Shore from Hawk Ridge at golden hour,
  550 feet above the lake. It's free, by the road, and the strongest
  romantic view in Duluth that the first Duluth journey doesn't already use.
- **The thread:** the long way home. Samuel Snively built the winding road
  up Amity Creek partly to reach his own house; the route follows the river
  up his road of stone bridges to the ridge, then back down to the lake.

  history, Brighton Beach, Lester Park); MnDOT's historic bridge records;
  the Hawk Ridge Bird Observatory's own pages (the count season, the best
  days, East Skyline's winter closure); Wikipedia on the Lester River;
  published histories of the Pickwick. Coordinates from OpenStreetMap and
  MnDOT; drives from Google's Routes API.
- **Uncertain:** "four of the original bridges still standing" and "about
  half" of the road's cost paid by Snively rest on published accounts, not
  on records seen first-hand; nobody's counted the bridges in person.
- **Hard-fail check:** none. Every stop is public and free; Skyline Parkway
  at Hawk Ridge is closed to cars from 1 December to 1 May, which Before
  You Go says plainly. The photos are real, of these places, licensed and
  credited.
- **Self-check scores:** distinctive discoveries 21/25, emotional arc and
  peak 22/25, route and practical 18/20, storytelling 13/15, hero and
  presentation 8/10, audience and mood fit 5/5. **87/100.** Held back by:
  no dinner to end on, and stop photos from mixed seasons.
- **Candidates turned down:**
  - *The Pickwick, for dinner, as the afterglow:* no licensed photo could

  - *Enger Tower at sunset as the peak:* already in the first Duluth
    journey.

## 4. Where Bob Dylan Began (`duluthCurious`)

- **Test group:** Duluth, couples only: the Curious one of three.
- **Status:** made and in the app (26 Nov 2026). Not yet walked.
- **Primary mood:** Curious (secondary: Cultural). **Audience:** a couple.
- **Tags:** Historical, Cultural, Hidden Gems.
- **Route:** the Duluth Depot (Departure) → the Clayton Jackson McGhie
  Memorial (Discovery) → Dylan's first home (Build) → the Duluth Armory

  steep climb (about 45 m) up North Third Avenue East.
- **Peak, and why:** the Armory, where a 17-year-old Robert Zimmerman stood
  near the stage as Buddy Holly played on 31 January 1959, three nights
  before Holly died, a night Dylan went back to accepting a Grammy in 1998
  and in his Nobel lecture. It's being restored, so the peak is from the
  street, and the story carries it.
- **The thread:** the city that made him: the railway town his family came
  to, the 1920 lynching his father is said to have lived near as a boy (and
  that "Desolation Row" is widely taken to be about), the duplex he was born
  into, and the night that set him going.
- **Checked against:** the Minnesota Historical Society's MNopedia and its
  Duluth lynchings site; the Equal Justice Initiative; Minnesota's Attorney
  General on Max Mason's 2020 pardon; Wikipedia and Zenith City on the
  Depot; the Current, MPR News and the Star Tribune on Bob Dylan Way (2006)
  and Dylan's Duluth; the Armory's own site (its restoration and 2027
  reopening); reporting on the 1959 concert. Coordinates from OpenStreetMap;
  walks from Google's Routes API.
- **Uncertain, and marked so in the journey:** the first home's house
  number (sources differ; 519 is the most common); that his father lived
  nearby in 1920 ("said to"); the "Desolation Row" link ("widely taken");
  the Armory's reopening year.
- **Hard-fail check:** none. The house is private and flagged as "from the
  street only"; the Armory is closed and says so. The memorial is handled
  plainly, with a quiet moment rather than a game. Photos licensed and
  credited; the Rose Garden's passed the photo check only at a quarter of
  the mood's strength.
- **Self-check scores:** distinctive discoveries 23/25, emotional arc and
  peak 21/25, route and practical 17/20, storytelling 15/15, hero and
  presentation 7/10, audience and mood fit 4/5. **87/100.** Held back by: a
  peak you can't go into until 2027, and a hero that's a plain brick hall.
- **Candidates turned down:** none beyond the places the first Duluth
  journey already uses.

Four journeys outside Tokyo, chosen to differ in every way the handoff asks
for: a rural valley (Ōhara, Kyoto), a historic city (Nara), an island

Photos: 22 from Wikimedia Commons (CC0, CC BY, CC BY-SA), each checked
against its Commons description or GPS. Commons now rate-limits full-size
downloads, so the last three were fetched at its standard 1920-pixel size,
and the pipeline now waits as long as Wikimedia asks before retrying. One

look alone (it was already very dark), so the Higashi Chaya stop reuses the
journey's hero photo of the same street instead, and that download was
dropped.

  Tourism Agency's English text, but other tellings give Ryōnin (the journey

## 7. Follow the Deer to the Great Buddha (`nara`)

- **Test group:** Japan, outside Tokyo: a historic city, for a family.
- **Status:** made and in the app (26 Nov 2026). Not yet walked.
- **Primary mood:** Playful (secondary: Curious). **Audience:** a family
  with children.
- **Tags:** Interactive, Historical, Cultural, Nature.
- **Route:** Gyōki's statue at Kintetsu Nara Station (Departure) → the deer
  on the Tōdai-ji approach (Discovery) → the Great South Gate (Build) → the
  Great Buddha Hall (Peak) → Nigatsu-dō (Afterglow). About 2.2 km on foot,

- **Peak, and why:** the Great Buddha in the largest wooden building in the
  world, and the pillar behind him with a hole the size of his nostril for
  children to crawl through (reopened on 1 June 2023, per Tōdai-ji). The

  famous sight.
- **The thread:** big things take everyone. Gyōki gathering a country's
  help, the god arriving on a deer, two giants carved by a team in 69 days,
  and a Buddha who lost his head and was rebuilt again and again by people
  asked to help.
- **Checked against:** Tōdai-ji's own site (history, 743 edict, casting
  747–749 in eight stages, the 855 earthquake, the fires of 1180 and 1567,
  Kōkei and 1692/1709, the Great South Gate and its guardians, Nigatsu-dō,
  hours, fees, photo rules, and its June 2023 notice reopening the pillar);
  the Japan Tourism Agency's Nara Park descriptions (about 1,300 deer, their
  bowing, the white deer legend, Kasuga Taisha). Coordinates from
  OpenStreetMap; walks from Google's Routes API; heights from GSI.
- **Uncertain, and marked so:** the price of deer crackers (about ¥200; not
  confirmed from an official source); Nigatsu-dō being open at all hours and
  free (from guides, not Tōdai-ji's own page); the Omizutori's exact dates
  (the journey says to check).
- **Licensing note:** Tōdai-ji asks that images of the temple aren't used
  for advertising or other commercial purposes. That's fine for testing,
  but the Great Buddha and guardian photos should be reconsidered before
  any commercial launch.
- **Hard-fail check:** none. The deer's risks (butting, nipping, mothers in
  summer, stags in autumn) are in Before You Go.
- **Self-check scores:** distinctive discoveries 22/25, emotional arc and
  peak 23/25, route and practical 19/20, storytelling 14/15, hero and
  presentation 8/10, audience and mood fit 5/5. **91/100.** Held back by:
  Nara is well known, and the guardians' photo is behind wire mesh.

  - *Kōfuku-ji's five-storey pagoda as the departure:* it's under

## 9. The City That Spent Its Gold on Beauty (`kanazawa`)

- **Test group:** Japan, outside Tokyo: a castle town, for a couple.
- **Status:** made and in the app (26 Nov 2026). Not yet walked.
- **Primary mood:** Cultural (secondary: Romantic). **Audience:** a couple.
- **Tags:** Cultural, Historical, Scenic, Hidden Gems.
- **Route:** Nagamachi (Departure) → Oyama Shrine (Discovery) →
  Kenroku-en (Build) → Higashi Chaya (Peak) → Kazuemachi and Kuragari-zaka
  (Afterglow). On foot, about 3.7 km, mostly flat. From about 1 PM into
  dusk.
- **Peak, and why:** Higashi Chaya as its lanterns come on, after an hour
  inside Ochaya Shima, a geiko teahouse from 1820. The most atmospheric,
  culturally rich place in Kanazawa, at its best time of day for two.
- **The thread:** the Maedas' wealth and what they spent it on: the
  richest domain in Japan, which chose beauty over arms so as not to worry
  the shogun. Their samurai's streets, the first lord and his wife, their
  garden and the "marsh of gold" legend, the gold leaf, the teahouses their
  merchants paid for, and the hill those merchants climbed at dusk.
- **Checked against:** the Japan Tourism Agency's descriptions (the Maeda
  family and the million koku, Nagamachi, Oyama Shrine, Kenroku-en,
  Higashi Chaya, Kazuemachi and Kuragari-zaka, Ishikawa's crafts and gold
  leaf); Ishikawa Prefecture's Kenroku-en page (hours, ¥320, free early
  entry); Kanazawa's tourism site (Shima's hours); listings for Shima (¥500)
  and the Nomura house (¥550, cash only); Japanese sources on Kinjō Reitaku
  beside Kanazawa Shrine. Coordinates from OpenStreetMap; walks from
  Google's Routes API; heights from GSI.
- **Uncertain, and marked so:** the Imohori Tōgorō story is told as a
  legend (sources tell it differently); Shima's hours differ between
